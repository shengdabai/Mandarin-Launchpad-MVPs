## Design Context

### Users

Adult Mandarin learners outside China, including self-directed beginners, intermediate learners whose listening lags behind reading, and heritage learners with strong passive knowledge but weak output. They open the product before a conversation, during a short daily study block, or after noticing a recurring mistake. Their job is not to “finish a course”; it is to understand and produce one more piece of real Chinese with confidence.

### Brand Personality

Clear, humane, evidence-led. The interface should feel like an editorial language lab: serious enough to trust, warm enough to return to, and never childish or gamified for its own sake.

### Aesthetic Direction

Revised 2026-10-02 (reskin): "cool study bench". Cool near-white page (no cream/paper, no ruled lines), white panels with 1px borders and 12px radius, a dark ink rail for navigation. Each of the ten micro-products owns one accent hue (set by `body[data-route]`, tokens in the `:root` block of `styles.css`) used for its primary button, labels and its inverted panel. Type: system sans for UI and headings (bold, light negative tracking), serif only for Hanzi glyphs, which stay large as working material. Shape: 8px controls, no pills. Navigation markers are Chinese glyphs, not numbers; context labels are sentence case, no tracking, no caps. Body and label text is 14px or larger.

Superseded: the earlier "warm paper neutrals" direction. Avoid reintroducing cream backgrounds, numbered labels, tracked uppercase eyebrows, mono labels, italic emphasis or pill buttons.

### Design Principles

1. Start from a learner failure, then offer one short action.
2. Show Chinese, pinyin, meaning, and context together only when each layer helps.
3. Prefer retrieval, comparison, recording, and reconstruction over passive reading.
4. Make progress legible without streak pressure or inflated scores.
5. Work locally, preserve privacy, support keyboard use, reduced motion, and mobile screens.
